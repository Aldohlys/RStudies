Reading principles
  1. Understand the present to evaluate possible scenarios. Read 00 (global movie) and 09 (panels) first; the scenario matches in 00 and 07 are only as good as that picture.
  2. Markets can diverge from fundamental value for long periods, but over a long enough horizon they revert to it. A warning, not a signal: over a BOT holding period, "overvalued" or "cheap" is no reason to fade a breakout or exit early. Stretched markets stay stretched until liquidity turns; watch principle 3, not valuation.
  3. Over short to intermediate horizons, prices follow liquidity rather than fundamental value, in both directions. When liquidity tightens (rising dollar, long yields, MOVE, wider credit: 02, 07, 09), the weakest go first (small caps, equal weight, breadth) and what money remains crowds into a few leaders, until everything gets sold together. When it loosens (falling dollar and real yields, tighter credit), participation broadens: equal weight and the Russell outperform and breadth widens (00).
  4. When everyone is in consensus, something different almost always happens. COT indices (11) and positioning stress (07) mark crowded trades; a crowded trade whose price stops following through is where the surprise comes from.
  5. Markets are not made of currencies, commodities, stocks and bonds, but of investors, traders and bankers who decide to buy or sell them. Ask who holds a position and who is forced to sell it (11, COT positioning by trader group), not only what the price did.
